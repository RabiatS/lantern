# Roadside

## First: get safe
Pull as far off the road as you can, onto the hard shoulder or a lay by. Hazard lights on. If you are on a motorway or fast road, get everyone out through the doors away from traffic and stand well behind the barrier, uphill of the car, never between the car and traffic. If you cannot get off the road or out safely, stay in the car with seatbelts on and call for help. At night or in fog, leave the sidelights on and wear anything reflective. Put a warning triangle about forty five metres behind the car on ordinary roads, not on a motorway.

## Calling for help with no signal
Move to higher ground or the top of a rise and try again. Text sometimes gets through when calls cannot. iPhone 14 and later can reach emergency services by satellite: dial the emergency number, and if the call fails, tap Emergency Text via Satellite and follow the on screen prompts to point the phone at the sky. Any phone can call the emergency number on another network's signal. If nothing works, a passing driver can be flagged down from a safe spot off the road.

## Car will not start: work through it
Dashboard lights dim or nothing at all: flat battery, see the jump start section. Lights fine but a rapid clicking when you turn the key: battery too weak to turn the engine. Engine turns over but never catches: could be out of fuel, a flooded engine, or a fault; wait a minute and try again with the pedal held down if the car is old. Key turns but nothing at all: check the car is in Park or Neutral, the steering lock is not jammed, and try the spare key. A push button car that will not respond may have a flat key fob battery; hold the fob against the start button.

## Jump start
Park the working car close, engines off, both in Park or Neutral, handbrakes on. Red lead: dead battery positive first, then good battery positive. Black lead: good battery negative, then the other end to bare unpainted metal on the dead car's engine block, away from the battery. Start the good car, let it run a few minutes, then start the dead car. Remove leads in reverse order. Drive the revived car for thirty minutes to charge. If it dies again, the battery or alternator needs replacing. Never let the clips touch each other or the wrong terminal, and never jump a cracked or leaking battery.

## Flat tyre
Get the car fully off the road on flat firm ground. Hazards on, handbrake on, in gear or Park. Loosen the wheel nuts a half turn before jacking. Jack at the marked point in the manual, raise until the tyre is clear, remove nuts and wheel, fit the spare, hand tighten nuts in a star pattern, lower the car, then tighten fully. A space saver spare is limited to about eighty kilometres an hour and a short distance. If you have a sealant kit instead, follow its instructions and get the tyre replaced soon. If it is not safe to change it where you are, do not; call for recovery.

## Warning lights
Red oil pressure light: stop the engine as soon as safely possible, do not drive on. Red temperature light or gauge in the red: pull over and stop, see overheating. Battery light: the car is running on the battery alone, drive straight to a safe place or a garage before it dies. Brake light: check the handbrake is off; if it stays on, stop and check fluid. Amber engine light: usually safe to continue gently and have it checked. Flashing engine light: stop soon, the engine may be damaging itself.

## Overheating
Pull over, engine off, bonnet up to let heat out. Do not open the coolant cap while the engine is hot; scalding steam. Wait at least thirty minutes. Then, with a cloth over the cap, open it slowly and top up with coolant or water if low. Check for a burst hose or a puddle underneath. If the level was fine, the fan or thermostat may have failed. Drive on only if the gauge stays normal, with the heater on full to pull heat from the engine.

## Out of fuel
Coast to safety with hazards on; power steering and brakes get heavy once the engine stops, so push harder. Do not walk along a motorway. Call recovery or a fuel delivery service. If you walk to a fuel station, take a proper fuel can. Diesel cars may need the system bled or many restarts after running dry; petrol cars usually start once fuel is added.

## Stuck in snow, mud or sand
Straighten the wheels. Clear snow or mud from in front of and behind the driving wheels. Put floor mats, sand, gravel or branches under the driving wheels. Use the highest gear that will pull away and the gentlest throttle; spinning wheels dig deeper. Rock the car by alternating forward and reverse in short moves. Turn traction control off for this only. If it is not working after a few tries, stop and call for help rather than burn out the clutch. In snow, keep the exhaust clear if you run the engine for heat.

## Locked out
Check every door and the boot. If keys are inside and the phone works, a recovery service or locksmith can open most cars without damage. A dealer can cut a key from the car's details. Do not break a window unless a child or animal is inside and in danger from heat; then break a window far from them.

## Brakes fail while driving
Pump the pedal quickly. Change down through the gears to slow the car. Apply the handbrake gradually, not all at once, holding the release button. Steer toward a safe stopping place, using an uphill slope, grass or a verge to slow further. Hazards and horn to warn others. Once stopped, do not drive again.

## Skids and aquaplaning
Ease off the throttle without braking hard. Look and steer where you want to go. If the rear slides, steer gently into the slide. When the tyres grip again, straighten. On a flooded road, drive slowly and steadily in a low gear and never enter water deeper than the centre of the wheel.

## Waiting for help
Stay where you told the recovery service you would be. Keep the phone warm and switch on low power mode; call once and text updates rather than calling repeatedly. In cold, stay in the car with a window slightly open and run the engine for ten minutes an hour only if the exhaust is clear. In heat, stay in shade and drink water. Keep children and pets with you. Do not accept a lift from a stranger unless it is an emergency, and tell someone where you are and who you are with.

## Breakdown kit worth carrying
Warning triangle, reflective vest, torch, jump leads or a jump pack, tyre inflator or sealant, water, a blanket, a phone charging cable and power bank, gloves, and a printed card with your recovery service number and the car's details.
